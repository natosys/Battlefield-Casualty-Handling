# Surgical Hours, Not Operating Theatres: Sizing the Land-Based Trauma System for Large Scale Combat Operations

## Abstract

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

**Background**

Casualty estimation is critical to the planning and design of the land-based trauma system deployed to support operations. Recent conflicts have produced lower casualty rates than those previously observed, or those expected in future large scale combat operations [[1]](#references), so a health system sized against recent experience is likely to not be adequate to handle the casualties of these more intense war fighting scenarios [[2]](#references).

**Objective**

To identify options to improve the land-based trauma system, to establish where that system fails as casualty intensity rises, and to state what the evidence supports for each lever a planner controls.

**Methods**

A discrete event simulation of a brigade force with aligned health assets was run at two casualty intensities derived from the FORECAS projection study [[3]](#references), a moderate intensity calibrated to the Falklands 1982 campaign and a high intensity calibrated to Okinawa 1945. Each lever the model can vary was then varied in a replicated experiment of 30 campaigns of 360 days. This paper reads those measurements as planning options. Every figure is quoted from the results paper [[4]](#references) and every experimental design is stated in the methods paper [[5]](#references).

**Conclusion**

The evidence supports acting now on the theatre evacuation policy, which can be lengthened to 30 days at the shipped establishment for moderate intensity, on the critical-care places the strategic airlift carries, and on the evacuation fleet. Surgical team hours, R2B holding beds, the pre-open hold window, the forward surgical saturation release and R2E intensive care beds are to be tested first, because their remedy is untested or their effect on outcomes unresolved. Moderate intensity needs margin in a few elements and a second forward surgical section. At high intensity the binding constraints are surgical hours at R2E, whose standing queue a fourth section shortens and with it the deaths of wounds, and critical-care airlift, and no establishment of beds or evacuation policy tested settles the system while the airlift backlog grows. Delivering post-operative intensive care forward is not supported, no forward holding arm resolving a change in deaths of wounds at high intensity and the arms moving in no consistent direction.

## Introduction

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

A planner designing a deployed health system faces a single problem with many parts: the system has to be sized before its deployment to support a campaign, against a casualty load nobody can know in advance. Optimising it means trading one part of the system against another, more surgical capacity against more holding beds, more evacuation lift against more in-theatre recovery, with a fixed establishment and a finite lift to move it.

Casualty volumes expected in large scale combat operations exceed those the deployed health systems of the past two decades were built around [[1]](#references), and planning assumptions carried forward from those operations understate both the volume and the acuity a peer fight would produce [[2]](#references). An establishment drawn from the last campaign is a baseline that may not transfer.

This paper reads the measurements of the results paper [[4]](#references) as planning options and states no measurement of its own. Every figure it quotes is regenerated from the tracked evidence, is tagged with the section of the results paper it comes from, and cannot differ from it. The experimental designs, the interval method and the replication counts are in the methods paper [[5]](#references). The first part below locates where the system fails as casualty intensity rises. The second gives a verdict on each element at each intensity and sizes the uplift where it is inadequate. The third sets every lever the simulation can evaluate in one table, with its cost and, for each intensity, its measurement and evidence label. The fourth turns the table into recommendations, split into those to act on now and those to test first, with a summary for planning against each intensity. The fifth states the effects the simulation could not resolve, and the sixth the research that would resolve them.

## The System and the Evidence Labels

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

The simulation is a discrete event model in which each casualty moves rearward through the echelons of allied medical support doctrine [[6]](#references): Role 1 (R1) primary care, R2B damage control surgery and short-term holding, R2E definitive surgery, intensive care and in-theatre recovery, and strategic aeromedical evacuation to a Role 4 national support base. The establishment is a combat brigade served by three R1 treatment teams, two R2B facilities and one R2E hospital. The system is described in full in the system reference [[7]](#references).

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  POI["Point of injury"] --> R1["R1 treatment team<br/>(3 teams)<br/>triage, resuscitation"]
  R1 -->|"surgical: theatre idle<br/>otherwise: holding below 80% full"| R2B["R2B facility (2)<br/>1 theatre, 1 surgical team<br/>12-hour roster<br/>5 holding beds each"]
  R1 -->|"no R2B facility qualifies"| R2E
  R2B -->|"team off shift or theatre busy"| R2E["R2E hospital (1)<br/>2 theatres, 3 surgical teams<br/>4 intensive care beds<br/>30 holding beds"]
  R2B -->|"recovered"| RTD["Return to duty"]
  R2E -->|"recovered in theatre"| RTD
  R2E -->|"recovery exceeds evacuation policy"| AME["Strategic aeromedical<br/>evacuation"]
  AME --> R4["Role 4<br/>national support base"]
```

Not every finding rests on the same strength of evidence. Each lever therefore carries a label saying what its evidence supports. A label attaches to a particular claim, so a lever whose diagnosis is measured may still have a remedy that is untested.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Measured** | The effect is established. Direction and approximate size are both supported; where the effect is a paired difference, it survives Holm's adjustment across every comparison of its experiment at a family-wise level of 5% ([Methods: Interval Methods](Methods.md#interval-methods)). |
| **Direction only** | The effect points the way expected and the mechanism is confirmed, but its size is not established. |
| **Unresolved** | The available compute could not separate the effect from random variation, including a paired difference whose interval excludes zero but which fails Holm's adjustment. Figures are bounds, not estimates. |
| **Untested** | The simulation cannot evaluate the option as presently built. |

Two readings of the results paper govern every statement below. Where two intervals overlap the simulation has not established a difference, and the gap between the two averages should not be acted on. And the interval describes how precisely an average is known, not how much one campaign varies: the campaigns themselves spread many times more widely than the average is uncertain, so a system sized against the average under-provides for the campaign that arrives ([Results: scenario totals](Results.md#comparative-scenario-analysis)).

## Where the Trauma System Fails First

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

**As casualty intensity rises the R2E beds fill, a standing queue forms for the surgical teams, and the critical-care airlift falls behind.** Wounded in action rise <!-- GEN cell:load_demand::Wounded in action/run::Ratio::mean -->3.61×<!-- /GEN --> from moderate to high intensity and the first surgeries performed at R2B and R2E <!-- GEN cell:load_demand::R2B and R2E first surgeries/run::Ratio::mean -->3.01×<!-- /GEN -->, a floor on the rise in surgical demand because saturated theatres cap the operations performed ([Results: demand, utilisation and shortfall per day](Results.md#demand-utilisation-and-shortfall-per-day)). Over the closing 90 days the R2E theatres are in use <!-- GEN cell:load_shortfall::R2E operating theatres::Moderate: utilisation::full -->28.1% [26.8%, 29.5%]<!-- /GEN --> of the time at moderate intensity and <!-- GEN cell:load_shortfall::R2E operating theatres::High: utilisation::mean -->75.9%<!-- /GEN --> at high, the holding beds <!-- GEN cell:load_shortfall::R2E holding beds::Moderate: utilisation::full -->99.6% [99.4%, 99.7%]<!-- /GEN --> and <!-- GEN cell:load_shortfall::R2E holding beds::High: utilisation::mean -->100.0%<!-- /GEN -->, and the intensive care beds <!-- GEN cell:load_shortfall::R2E intensive care::Moderate: utilisation::full -->86.8% [86.0%, 87.6%]<!-- /GEN --> and <!-- GEN cell:load_shortfall::R2E intensive care::High: utilisation::mean -->97.5%<!-- /GEN -->. The beds do not fall behind, because a casualty who finds none free is cared for in overflow: the holding queue grows by <!-- GEN cell:load_shortfall::R2E holding beds::High: shortfall per day::full -->0.002 [−0.022, 0.027]<!-- /GEN --> casualties a day at high intensity and the post-operative overflow by <!-- GEN cell:load_shortfall::R2E post-operative overflow::High: shortfall per day::full -->−0.001 [−0.004, 0.002]<!-- /GEN -->, both intervals containing zero. Nor does the theatre queue, whose growth of <!-- GEN cell:load_shortfall::R2E operating theatres::High: shortfall per day::full -->0.014 [−0.151, 0.179]<!-- /GEN --> casualties a day also contains zero, but it settles with a casualty almost always waiting. What falls behind is the casualties awaiting airlift, the staging overflow growing by <!-- GEN cell:load_shortfall::R2E staging overflow::High: shortfall per day::full -->7.692 [7.514, 7.871]<!-- /GEN -->. A full bed pool is not a failing one, but every casualty in overflow is receiving a lesser standard of care, so the beds' shortfall is paid in care quality rather than in a queue. A ratio of closing-window queues between the intensities, such as <!-- GEN cell:scenario_queue::R2E operating theatre::Ratio::full -->75.09×<!-- /GEN --> for the theatres, measures how much longer the standing queue is at high intensity, not a rate at which it grows ([Results: comparative scenario analysis](Results.md#comparative-scenario-analysis)). **Evidence: measured.**

**Surgical team scheduling is the principal constraint.** A casualty takes an operating theatre before taking one of the surgical teams that staff it, so a theatre stands occupied while the casualty inside it waits for a team. The theatres are available around the clock and the teams work 12-hour shifts. Adding theatres would therefore not relieve a queue that measures a wait for people. The R2E theatre queue at moderate intensity averages <!-- GEN cell:scenario_queue::R2E operating theatre::Moderate intensity mean queue::mean -->0.967<!-- /GEN --> casualties, so the establishment is not comfortably adequate even at the lower rate ([Results: comparative scenario analysis](Results.md#comparative-scenario-analysis)). In the verified seed-42 campaign the single second-shift section carried the whole night-time surgical load and was busier and queued longer than the first-shift sections ([Results: Annex A](Results.md#annex-a-model-verification-of-one-seed-42-campaign)). **Evidence: measured.**

**The same shortfall takes a different form at each intensity.** At high intensity the R2E theatre queue stands empty for <!-- GEN cell:queue_clearance::R2E operating theatres::High: queue empty::mean -->7%<!-- /GEN --> of the campaign and its longest unbroken run above zero, in days with the interquartile range, is <!-- GEN cell:queue_clearance::R2E operating theatres::High: longest run above zero (days)::full -->132.4 (65.8–167.8)<!-- /GEN --> days; at moderate intensity it stands empty for <!-- GEN cell:queue_clearance::R2E operating theatres::Moderate: queue empty::mean -->85%<!-- /GEN --> and its longest run is <!-- GEN cell:queue_clearance::R2E operating theatres::Moderate: longest run above zero (days)::full -->4.3 (3.4–5.9)<!-- /GEN --> days ([Results: queue behaviour over the campaign](Results.md#queue-behaviour-over-the-campaign)). A queue that clears is a surge problem, answered by capability brought to bear on the heavy day and stood down afterwards. A queue that never clears is an establishment problem, and no surge capability reaches it because there is no trough to surge into. Surgical hours are therefore a standing measure at high intensity and a surge measure at moderate, and a force sized for one is not sized for the other. **Evidence: measured.**

**At high intensity the theatre queue settles at a standing level and the airlift backlog has none.** Over a 360-day campaign the R2E theatre queue is classified as <!-- GEN cell:long_horizon_stability::R2E operating theatre queue::High intensity::full -->converged at 71.0<!-- /GEN --> (a level, or for a drifting response the drift per 30-day block, then the first and last block means), while the staging overflow is classified <!-- GEN cell:long_horizon_stability::R2E staging overflow census::High intensity::full -->**drifting, +11.2%/block**, 117.7 to 2,739.6<!-- /GEN --> and the strategic evacuation backlog <!-- GEN cell:long_horizon_stability::Strategic evacuation backlog::High intensity::full -->**drifting, +11.6%/block**, 137.5 to 2,927.6<!-- /GEN -->, and the holding queue and the post-operative overflow converge. At moderate intensity the theatre and holding queues converge, while the post-operative overflow, the staging overflow and the airlift backlog drift slowly (<!-- GEN cell:long_horizon_stability::R2E post-operative overflow census::Moderate intensity::full -->converged at 5.54<!-- /GEN -->, <!-- GEN cell:long_horizon_stability::R2E staging overflow census::Moderate intensity::full -->converged at 30.1<!-- /GEN --> and <!-- GEN cell:long_horizon_stability::Strategic evacuation backlog::Moderate intensity::full -->**drifting, +4.3%/block**, 26.1 to 44.6<!-- /GEN -->) ([Results: sustained operations](Results.md#sustained-operations)). A high-intensity airlift figure is therefore a point on a growing backlog, and a moderate-intensity one a level still rising slowly at the end of a year. **Evidence: measured.**

**Intensive care is rationed rather than queued.** When R2E intensive care is full the simulation places an operated casualty in a holding bed, or in overflow when the holding beds are full too, and defers other casualties' surgery until a bed is free, so the shortfall appears as casualties receiving a lesser standard of care rather than as a queue. Over the closing 90 days a mean of <!-- GEN cell:scenario_queue::R2E post-operative overflow census::Moderate intensity mean queue::mean -->5.724<!-- /GEN --> operated casualties are cared for in post-operative overflow at moderate intensity and <!-- GEN cell:scenario_queue::R2E post-operative overflow census::High intensity mean queue::mean -->19.794<!-- /GEN --> at high, and the share of post-definitive care delivered in a holding bed is <!-- GEN cell:degraded_care::High intensity: Post-definitive care::Whole campaign::mean -->88.8%<!-- /GEN --> at high intensity against <!-- GEN cell:degraded_care::Moderate intensity: Post-definitive care::Whole campaign::mean -->65.0%<!-- /GEN --> at moderate ([Results: queue behaviour over the campaign](Results.md#queue-behaviour-over-the-campaign)). A rate that is a standing condition from the first week is answered only by the establishment, not by a reserve brought forward on particular days. **Evidence: measured.**

**A zero R2B theatre queue signals a shortfall being exported, not spare capacity.** The routing policy moves a casualty requiring surgery to R2E whenever the R2B theatre is occupied or its team is off shift beyond the pre-open window, so no queue can form there ([Results: comparative scenario analysis](Results.md#comparative-scenario-analysis)). Any measure of whether R2B is adequately resourced has to count what was sent rearward, not what waited.

**Planning for moderate intensity needs margin in a few elements and a second forward surgical section; planning for high intensity needs surgical hours and critical-care airlift that the levers tested do not supply.** A force sized for moderate intensity needs no uplift of the R2E theatres or holding beds, can lengthen its evacuation policy to 30 days without adding beds, should hold margin at the intensive care beds and the critical-care airlift seats, the beds running nearly full and the backlog behind the seats rising slowly, and should field a second surgical section per R2B unit if the routing gap matters. ## Every Lever, and What the Evidence Supports

## Recommendations in Priority Order

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

**Recommendations are split by whether the remedy is measured, and ranked within each group by how directly the measured effect improves the health outcome of the force.** Act now holds the levers whose effect is measured and resolved against a comparator. Test first holds the levers whose diagnosis is measured but whose remedy is untested or whose effect on outcomes is unresolved. Within a group a lever that moves returns to duty, deaths of wounds or access to intensive care ranks above one that moves only a queue, and a lever addressing the principal constraint ranks above one better measured but acting where the system is not failing.

### Act Now

#### 3. Extend Surgical Team Coverage Towards 24 Hours

**Surgical team hours are the capacity to add, at both R2B and R2E, and the theatres to use them already exist.** **Evidence for the diagnosis: measured. Evidence for the effect of added sections: measured. Evidence for the best means of sourcing the hours: untested.**

### What Resourcing Alone Does Not Fix, and What Is Not Supported

**Forward holding is not supported on the present evidence.** Holding operated casualties forward at R2B does not measurably relieve R2E intensive care at moderate intensity: forward utilisation rises across the windows because the policy is being applied, while the post-operative overflow at R2E is not separable from the arm with none held forward ([Results: forward holding](Results.md#forward-holding-of-post-operative-intensive-care)). At high intensity no forward arm resolves a change in deaths of wounds per run, and the arms move in no consistent direction: <!-- GEN cell:forward_hold_high::Off (current)::Died of wounds per run::full -->568.60 [500.55, 636.65]<!-- /GEN --> with none held forward, <!-- GEN cell:forward_hold_high::240 min::Died of wounds per run::full -->573.00 [493.77, 652.23]<!-- /GEN --> at a 240-minute window, <!-- GEN cell:forward_hold_high::1,440 min::Died of wounds per run::full -->573.03 [504.20, 641.86]<!-- /GEN --> at 1,440 minutes and <!-- GEN cell:forward_hold_high::Capacity only::Died of wounds per run::full -->614.50 [565.71, 663.29]<!-- /GEN --> with the capacity trigger alone, every interval overlapping ([Results: forward holding](Results.md#forward-holding-of-post-operative-intensive-care)). **Evidence: unresolved at both intensities.** Enlarging the R2E holding pool alone is a different matter: the residual queue of <!-- GEN cell:establishment::R2E hold mean queue::30 beds (shipped)::mean -->24.38<!-- /GEN --> at 30 beds is <!-- GEN cell:establishment::R2E hold mean queue::45 beds::mean -->18.71<!-- /GEN --> at 45 ([Results: R2E holding establishment](Results.md#r2e-holding-establishment)), a settled queue at both, so the beds buy a shorter wait for a holding bed rather than stability. **Evidence: measured.**

**Do not apply forward holding under sustained saturation, and treat the rationing gate as unresolved.** At high intensity the rationing gate changes deaths of wounds by <!-- GEN cell:icu_gate_high::Died of wounds per run::Paired difference::full -->−29.33 [−138.46, +79.79]<!-- /GEN --> per run, an interval that contains zero ([Results: post-operative intensive care gate](Results.md#post-operative-intensive-care-gate)), and forward holding resolves no change. Both effects arise partly from the relative mortality penalties the model applies to a casualty held outside a full intensive care bed (`r2b_icu_penalty` and `r2e_postop_hold_penalty`), which are informed estimates, so their direction is set inside the model rather than discovered by it. **Evidence: unresolved, and set partly inside the model.**

**Three conditions are properties of the design, not of any resource level.** First, the intensive care rationing gate relieves load, from <!-- GEN cell:icu_gate::R2E ICU utilisation (%)::Without the rule::mean -->85.9<!-- /GEN -->% utilisation without it to <!-- GEN cell:icu_gate::R2E ICU utilisation (%)::With the rule::mean -->86.8<!-- /GEN -->% with it, and at moderate intensity its mortality cost is not established ([Results: post-operative intensive care gate](Results.md#post-operative-intensive-care-gate)). The casualties recovering in a holding bed and in an intensive care bed die at rates the pathway counts do not separate, <!-- GEN cell:icu_gate_pathways::Holding bed::Rate::mean -->0.37%<!-- /GEN --> against <!-- GEN cell:icu_gate_pathways::Intensive care bed::Rate::mean -->0.45%<!-- /GEN -->, the holding-bed rate resting on a handful of deaths, and any difference the model shows between them is built into it rather than discovered by it. Second, a casualty surge event degrades care without exposing a new constraint: event casualties die of wounds at <!-- GEN cell:casualty_surge::Died-of-wounds rate, event casualties::Events injected::mean -->2.41%<!-- /GEN --> against <!-- GEN cell:casualty_surge::Died-of-wounds rate, ordinary casualties::Events injected::mean -->1.31%<!-- /GEN --> for ordinary casualties in the same campaigns, and the events also raise the ordinary rate from <!-- GEN cell:casualty_surge::Died-of-wounds rate, ordinary casualties::No events injected::mean -->0.39%<!-- /GEN --> to <!-- GEN cell:casualty_surge::Died-of-wounds rate, ordinary casualties::Events injected::mean -->1.31%<!-- /GEN --> ([Results: casualty surge events](Results.md#casualty-surge-events)). The theatre is the first pool to move: the peak R2E theatre queue rises from <!-- GEN cell:casualty_surge_size::None::Peak R2E theatre queue::full -->33.7 ± 6.3<!-- /GEN --> with no events to <!-- GEN cell:casualty_surge_size::20::Peak R2E theatre queue::full -->39.7 ± 9.8<!-- /GEN --> at 20 casualties per event and <!-- GEN cell:casualty_surge_size::40::Peak R2E theatre queue::full -->57.9 ± 6.1<!-- /GEN --> at 40, while the post-operative overflow moves from <!-- GEN cell:casualty_surge_size::None::Peak R2E post-operative overflow census::full -->27.1 ± 0.8<!-- /GEN --> to <!-- GEN cell:casualty_surge_size::40::Peak R2E post-operative overflow census::full -->30.6 ± 0.5<!-- /GEN --> ([Results: casualty surge event size](Results.md#casualty-surge-event-size)). Policies to recover holding capacity during an event are untested. Third, the high-intensity airlift backlog drifts without a level, so no establishment drawn from a high-intensity closing-window figure of it is a steady-state sizing ([Results: sustained operations](Results.md#sustained-operations)).

Three kinds of limit separate them. The hold window's downstream effects on R2E load are limited by the stream divergence between its arms, which grows with the campaign length, so lengthening the campaign did not resolve them and the replication counts above are far beyond what any horizon change buys. ## Research Agenda

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

**The development priorities follow from the levers the simulation cannot yet evaluate or resolve.** Each item below names the decision it would unblock.

| Priority | Development | Decision it unblocks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost surgical sections in personnel and sustainment, and sweep the R2E theatre count beside the section count | Whether the sections that shorten the high-intensity standing queue are affordable, and whether a third theatre is then needed |
| 2 | Sweep the critical-care places per sortie beside the sortie interval at both intensities, and model a critical casualty's deterioration while awaiting airlift | Whether configuring the airlift for critical care stabilises the casualties awaiting evacuation, the binding constraint at high intensity and the thin margin at moderate, and how many deaths the wait causes |
| 3 | Re-run the unresolved experiments at the replication counts the resolution table states | The hold window's effect on R2E load, and the cost of rationing and of the release |
| 4 | Sweep the R2B diversion thresholds across their range | Where to strike the trade between waiting forward and transferring load rearward |
| 5 | Test policies for recovering holding capacity during a casualty surge event, and the triggers for applying them | How to relieve the reversal of the intensive care and holding pathways under surge |
| 6 | Casualty severity conditioning of surgery durations | Whether theatre contention is understated on the heavy days it is measured on |
| 7 | A global screen with common random numbers and a design sized to the replication noise, and a sizing of the R2E establishment against the level its queues reach under a defined surge | Whether parameters interact in ways the one-lever sweeps cannot detect, and whether an informed estimate not yet swept could move a finding ([system reference](../README.md#further-development)) |
| 8 | Model clinicians individually rather than as whole teams ([system reference](../README.md#further-development)) | Whether team-level seizure overstates the scarcity of surgical and intensive care staff, and which role binds first |

Alongside these, the simulated system's design and calibration would benefit from structured review by clinical and health planning subject matter experts. The parameters governing intensive care rationing and post-operative risk are informed estimates rather than measured values, and expert calibration would do more to improve confidence in the mortality findings than additional computation.

## Limitations

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

Five limitations bear on how the recommendations above should be read.

**The simulation is verified but not validated.** It behaves as its specification describes, which is a separate question from whether that specification represents the real trauma system well [[9]](#references). Verification is reported in [Annex A of the results paper](Results.md#annex-a-model-verification-of-one-seed-42-campaign). The treated-cohort died-of-wounds rates are reported in [Results](Results.md#treated-cohort-mortality) at each anchor's horizon and at 360 days: the shipped configuration's rate is <!-- GEN cell:dow_calibration::Shipped default::Rate at the anchor's horizon::full -->0.421% [0.350%, 0.492%]<!-- /GEN --> at the 30 days its Ajax Bay anchor describes and <!-- GEN cell:dow_calibration::Shipped default::Rate at 360 days::full -->0.541% [0.453%, 0.629%]<!-- /GEN --> at 360 days, because under a model in which every hour of delay carries risk a longer campaign accumulates more of it. Every lever above is a lever inside the simulation, and holds only as far as the simulation does.

**Compute time limited what could be measured.** The effects in the resolution table are unresolved for that reason, for the rarity of the event, or because they fail the Holm adjustment that guards against reading one chance difference in twenty as real, and the replication counts that would settle them are stated there. This constrains the precision of the findings rather than their direction, and it bears hardest on mortality, which is the rarest quantity the simulation reports.

**Comparisons between two configurations are not controlled.** Changing a setting alters the sequence of random draws, so the two arms of a comparison generate different casualty streams and cannot be matched campaign for campaign; their responses are uncorrelated within replication ([Methods: Pairing Arms on a Control Seed](Methods.md#pairing-arms-on-a-control-seed)). The effect is a loss of precision rather than a bias, which is why several comparisons are unresolved at the replication counts used. The casualty intensity comparison is unaffected, its arms differing by design rather than by a small perturbation.

**The high-intensity airlift figures measure a backlog still growing, not a level.** The sustained-horizon measurement shows the casualties awaiting airlift still growing at the end of a year, and at moderate intensity rising slowly ([Results: sustained operations](Results.md#sustained-operations)), so no airlift figure is a steady-state figure. Nothing recommended here depends on that level, the conclusion drawn being that the backlog does not clear. The high-intensity theatre queue, by contrast, settles at a standing level.

**High intensity is a projection of a force that does not adapt.** The establishment, the surgical roster and the weekly airlift stay as configured however far demand outruns them, with no expectant category, no surgical reinforcement and no airlift added (README Further Development L45). The surgical and evacuation queues therefore keep building, deaths of wounds per day are classified <!-- GEN cell:long_horizon_stability::Deaths of wounds per day::High intensity::full -->**drifting, +5.1%/block**, 0.5 to 2.2<!-- /GEN -->, and the treated-cohort rate is <!-- GEN cell:dow_calibration::High intensity::Rate at the anchor's horizon::full -->2.905% [2.681%, 3.128%]<!-- /GEN --> at the 82 days of the Okinawa anchor against <!-- GEN cell:dow_calibration::High intensity::Rate at 360 days::full -->5.692% [5.088%, 6.295%]<!-- /GEN --> at 360 days ([Results: treated-cohort mortality](Results.md#treated-cohort-mortality); [sustained operations](Results.md#sustained-operations)). A high-intensity mortality figure is the cost of a year without adapting, not the expected outcome of a high-intensity campaign; the surgical section and sortie interval sweeps measure what two forms of adaptation buy ([Results: surgical team establishment](Results.md#surgical-team-establishment)).

**The risk of waiting for airlift is understated.** A critical casualty awaiting a seat is held in a holding bed, or in overflow when none is free, at the death rate of post-operative intensive care, for however long the wait lasts. At high intensity those waits run to months, and a Priority 1 surgical casualty held that long would in reality deteriorate or need further surgery, so the deaths of wounds the airlift shortfall causes in the model are a floor: <!-- GEN cell:scenario_totals::Died of wounds/run::High intensity::mean -->568.60<!-- /GEN --> per run at high intensity against <!-- GEN cell:scenario_totals::Died of wounds/run::Moderate intensity::mean -->21.17<!-- /GEN --> at moderate ([Results](Results.md#comparative-scenario-analysis)). No open source gives the hazard of that situation.

Two narrower caveats apply. Clinical teams are taken whole rather than by individual clinician, so team utilisation overstates scarcity where a procedure needs only part of a team; and one pool of R2E holding beds carries both in-theatre recovery and the strategic evacuation wait, so those queue figures combine two demands. The model's gaps are catalogued in the system reference [[7]](#references).

## Conclusion

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

This paper set out to identify options for improving the land-based trauma system, to establish where that system fails first as casualty intensity rises, and to state what the evidence supports for each lever a planner controls.

**The system does not scale from moderate to high casualty intensity: the R2E beds fill, a standing queue forms for the surgical teams, and the critical-care airlift falls behind.** Wounded in action rise <!-- GEN cell:load_demand::Wounded in action/run::Ratio::mean -->3.61×<!-- /GEN --> and first surgeries <!-- GEN cell:load_demand::R2B and R2E first surgeries/run::Ratio::mean -->3.01×<!-- /GEN -->, after which the R2E holding and intensive care beds run full and the theatre queue stands at <!-- GEN cell:scenario_queue::R2E operating theatre::High intensity mean queue::mean -->72.616<!-- /GEN --> casualties ([Results: demand, utilisation and shortfall per day](Results.md#demand-utilisation-and-shortfall-per-day)). The theatre constraint is surgical team scheduling rather than theatre space, and it takes a different form at each intensity: at high intensity the theatre queue stands empty for <!-- GEN cell:queue_clearance::R2E operating theatres::High: queue empty::mean -->7%<!-- /GEN --> of the campaign, and a fourth R2E section takes deaths of wounds from <!-- GEN cell:surgical_team_high::Died of wounds::R2E 3, R2B 1 (shipped)::mean -->568.60<!-- /GEN --> to <!-- GEN cell:surgical_team_high::Died of wounds::R2E 4, R2B 1::mean -->308.70<!-- /GEN --> per run ([Results: surgical team establishment](Results.md#surgical-team-establishment)), while at moderate intensity it clears for <!-- GEN cell:queue_clearance::R2E operating theatres::Moderate: queue empty::mean -->85%<!-- /GEN --> of it ([Results: queue behaviour over the campaign](Results.md#queue-behaviour-over-the-campaign)). The airlift constraint is the critical-care places, which the standard places cannot substitute for.

**The recommendations are split by whether the remedy is measured.** Act now: hold the evacuation policy as a decision and read its price from the trade table, the largest policy holding the R2E pools at moderate intensity being <!-- GEN cell:policy_grid_largest_moderate::30::Largest policy holding the R2E pools::full -->30 d<!-- /GEN --> at the shipped 30 beds ([Results](Results.md#moderate-intensity-evacuation-policy-and-holding-establishment)); configure the strategic airlift for critical care, whose places collapse in <!-- GEN cell:airlift_collapse::Critical, 0%::Collapsed::full -->1 of 30<!-- /GEN --> moderate-intensity campaigns with no sortie lost and <!-- GEN cell:airlift_collapse_high::Critical, 0%::Collapsed::full -->30 of 30<!-- /GEN --> at high intensity while the standard places fly part-empty ([Results: national support base and strategic airlift](Results.md#national-support-base-and-strategic-airlift)); keep three ambulances, four at high intensity, and four trucks, noting that the R2E evacuation sections and the truck fleet stand largely idle. Test first: surgical team coverage towards 24 hours, a fourth R2E section shortening the high-intensity theatre queue and a second R2B section per unit removing the off-shift diversions, because the means of sourcing the hours is untested; the pre-open window, which keeps <!-- GEN cell:hold_window::Casualties held at R2B::Window 60 min::mean -->106.57<!-- /GEN --> casualties forward per campaign without a resolved effect on R2E load ([Results: R2B pre-open hold window](Results.md#r2b-pre-open-hold-window)); the forward surgical saturation release; and intensive care beds, which improve the care pathway without a resolved outcome effect. R2B holding beds have no forward queue to relieve. Do not deliver post-operative intensive care forward under sustained saturation: at high intensity no forward holding arm resolved a change in deaths of wounds, which ran at <!-- GEN cell:forward_hold_high::Off (current)::Died of wounds per run::mean -->568.60<!-- /GEN --> with none held forward, <!-- GEN cell:forward_hold_high::120 min::Died of wounds per run::mean -->530.53<!-- /GEN --> at a 120-minute window and <!-- GEN cell:forward_hold_high::240 min + capacity::Died of wounds per run::mean -->615.83<!-- /GEN --> with a 240-minute window and the capacity trigger, with overlapping intervals and partly through informed-estimate penalties ([Results: forward holding](Results.md#forward-holding-of-post-operative-intensive-care)); the rationing gate's effect is unresolved at <!-- GEN cell:icu_gate_high::Died of wounds per run::Paired difference::mean -->−29.33<!-- /GEN --> ([Results: post-operative intensive care gate](Results.md#post-operative-intensive-care-gate)). Enlarging the R2E holding pool shortens the wait for a holding bed but is not needed for stability at moderate intensity.

**Further research would be worthwhile, and in three directions.** The most valuable is pricing the surgical section establishment against the holding capacity lever in sustainment as well as personnel, the lever table stating only personnel and principal equipment. The second is the lift of critical casualties, the share of each airframe configured for critical care being the lever the model does not yet sweep, and which carried the residual wait at a three-day sortie interval (<!-- GEN cell:interval_grid_high_wait::3 d interval, 21 d policy, 30 beds::Mean wait, critical channel (days)::mean -->22.44<!-- /GEN --> days against <!-- GEN cell:interval_grid_high_wait::3 d interval, 21 d policy, 30 beds::Mean wait, standard channel (days)::mean -->1.51<!-- /GEN --> on the standard channel, [Results](Results.md#high-intensity-strategic-sortie-interval-by-policy-and-holding-establishment)), together with the repeated analysis of the effects the resolution table leaves open. The third is structured expert review of the simulated system's design and calibration, which would do more for confidence in the mortality findings than any amount of additional computation.

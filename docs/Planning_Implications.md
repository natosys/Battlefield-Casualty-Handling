# Surgical Hours Before Operating Theatres: Sizing the Land-Based Trauma System for Large Scale Combat Operations

## Abstract

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

**Background**

Casualty estimation is critical to the planning and design of the land-based trauma system deployed to support operations. Recent conflicts have produced lower casualty rates than those previously observed, or those expected in future large scale combat operations [[1]](#references), so a health system sized against recent experience is likely to not be adequate to handle the casualties of these more intense war fighting scenarios [[2]](#references).

**Objective**

To identify options to improve the land-based trauma system, to establish where that system fails as casualty intensity rises, and to state what the evidence supports for each lever a planner controls.

**Methods**

A discrete event simulation of a brigade force with aligned health assets was run at two casualty intensities derived from the FORECAS projection study [[3]](#references), a moderate intensity calibrated to the Falklands 1982 campaign and a high intensity calibrated to Okinawa 1945. Each lever the model can vary was then varied in a replicated experiment of 30 campaigns of 360 days. This paper reads those measurements as planning options. Every figure is quoted from the results paper [[4]](#references) and every experimental design is stated in the methods paper [[5]](#references).

**Results**

At moderate intensity the shipped system keeps pace: every queue settles over a 360-day campaign and about <!-- GEN cell:scenario_totals::Died of wounds/run::Moderate intensity::dp0 -->21<!-- /GEN --> casualties die of wounds, but the R2E beds run full and <!-- GEN cell:degraded_care::Moderate intensity: Post-definitive care::Whole campaign::dp0 -->65%<!-- /GEN --> of the intensive care owed after definitive surgery is given in a holding bed ([Results](Results.md#queue-behaviour-over-the-campaign)). At high intensity wounded in action rise <!-- GEN cell:scenario_totals::Wounded in action/run::Ratio::sig2 -->3.6×<!-- /GEN --> and two parts of the system fall behind: the R2E surgical teams, with about <!-- GEN cell:scenario_queue::R2E operating theatre::High intensity mean queue::dp0 -->73<!-- /GEN --> casualties waiting and <!-- GEN cell:scenario_totals::Time to first incision (hours)::High intensity::dp0 -->71<!-- /GEN --> hours to first incision ([Results](Results.md#comparative-scenario-analysis)), and the critical-care places of the strategic airlift, whose demand is <!-- GEN cell:airlift_collapse_high::Critical, 0%::Mean utilisation::full -->2.65<!-- /GEN --> times the places flown ([Results](Results.md#national-support-base-and-strategic-airlift)). A fourth R2E surgical team prevents about <!-- GEN cell:surgical_team_paired_high::Died of wounds::R2E 4, R2B 1::abs_sig2 -->260<!-- /GEN --> deaths of wounds per campaign and a second team per R2B unit about <!-- GEN cell:surgical_team_paired_high::Died of wounds::R2E 3, R2B 2::abs_sig2 -->220<!-- /GEN --> ([Results](Results.md#surgical-team-establishment)); configuring 45 of the 90 places for critical care and flying every three days keeps the airlift level and prevents about <!-- GEN cell:airlift_places_paired_high::Died of wounds::45/45, 3 days::abs_dp0 -->176<!-- /GEN --> ([Results](Results.md#critical-care-places-per-sortie)). Added surgery moves the pressure onto the post-operative beds, which more R2E intensive care beds take back and more holding beds do not ([Results](Results.md#surgery-and-post-operative-beds-together)), and a delay in forward evacuation costs lives forward that no capacity further back recovers ([Results](Results.md#forward-evacuation-delay)). The airlift decides when the national support base receives its load, not how large it is.

**Conclusion**

The shipped land-based trauma system copes with a moderate-intensity campaign at a degraded standard of care and fails at high intensity, where surgical team hours at R2E and the critical-care places of the strategic airlift are the two constraints that decide the outcome. The evidence supports acting now on surgical teams at R2E and R2B, planned together with the R2E intensive care beds behind them; on more critical-care airlift places flown more often; on the theatre evacuation policy as a command trade; and on the ambulance fleet. Holding beds, the pre-open hold window, the forward surgical saturation release and intensive care beds beyond those the added surgery needs are to be tested first. Every finding assumes forward evacuation is uncontested, and holds when the two most uncertain assumptions about the casualties are relaxed.

## Introduction

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

A planner designing a deployed health system faces a single problem with many parts: the system has to be sized before its deployment to support a campaign, against a casualty load nobody can know in advance. Optimising it means trading one part of the system against another, more surgical capacity against more holding beds, more evacuation lift against more in-theatre recovery, with a fixed establishment and a finite lift to move it.

Casualty volumes expected in large scale combat operations exceed those the deployed health systems of the past two decades were built around [[1]](#references), and planning assumptions carried forward from those operations understate both the volume and the acuity a peer fight would produce [[2]](#references). An establishment drawn from the last campaign is a baseline that may not transfer.

This paper reads the measurements of the results paper [[4]](#references) as planning options and states no measurement of its own. Every figure it quotes is regenerated from the tracked evidence, is tagged with the section of the results paper it comes from, and cannot differ from it. The experimental designs, the interval method and the replication counts are in the methods paper [[5]](#references). The first part below states nine findings, each with the table and figure behind it: whether the system keeps pace at each intensity, the two constraints that decide the outcome, and what the levers a planner controls buy. The second sets every lever the simulation can evaluate in one table, with its cost and, for each intensity, its measurement and evidence label. The third turns the findings into recommendations, split into those to act on now and those to test first, with a summary for planning against each intensity. The fourth states the effects the simulation could not resolve, and the fifth the research that would resolve them. An annex gives a verdict on each element at each intensity.

## The System and the Evidence Labels

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

The simulation is a discrete event model in which each casualty moves rearward through the echelons of allied medical support doctrine [[6]](#references): Role 1 (R1) primary care, R2B damage control surgery and short-term holding, R2E definitive surgery, intensive care and in-theatre recovery, and strategic aeromedical evacuation to a Role 4 national support base. The establishment is a combat brigade served by three R1 treatment teams, two R2B facilities and one R2E hospital. The system is described in full in the system reference [[7]](#references).

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  POI["Point of injury"] --> R1["R1 treatment team<br/>(3 teams)<br/>triage, resuscitation"]
  R1 -->|"surgical: theatre idle<br/>otherwise: holding below 80% full"| R2B["R2B facility (2)<br/>1 theatre, 1 surgical team<br/>12-hour roster<br/>5 holding beds each"]
  R1 -->|"no R2B facility qualifies"| R2E
  R2B -->|"team off shift or theatre busy"| R2E["R2E hospital (1)<br/>2 theatres, 3 surgical teams<br/>4 intensive care beds<br/>30 holding beds"]
  R2B -->|"recovered"| RTD["Return to duty"]
  R2E -->|"recovered in theatre"| RTD
  R2E -->|"recovery exceeds evacuation policy"| AME["Strategic aeromedical<br/>evacuation"]
  AME --> R4["Role 4<br/>national support base"]
```

Not every finding rests on the same strength of evidence. Each lever therefore carries a label saying what its evidence supports. A label attaches to a particular claim, so a lever whose diagnosis is measured may still have a remedy that is untested.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Measured** | The effect is established. Direction and approximate size are both supported; where the effect is a paired difference, it survives Holm's adjustment across every comparison of its experiment at a family-wise level of 5% ([Methods: Interval Methods](Methods.md#interval-methods)). |
| **Direction only** | The effect points the way expected and the mechanism is confirmed, but its size is not established. |
| **Unresolved** | The available compute could not separate the effect from random variation, including a paired difference whose interval excludes zero but which fails Holm's adjustment. Figures are bounds, not estimates. |
| **Untested** | The simulation cannot evaluate the option as presently built. |

Two readings of the results paper govern every statement below. Where two intervals overlap the simulation has not established a difference, and the gap between the two averages should not be acted on. And the interval describes how precisely an average is known, not how much one campaign varies: the campaigns themselves spread many times more widely than the average is uncertain, so a system sized against the average under-provides for the campaign that arrives ([Results: scenario totals](Results.md#comparative-scenario-analysis)).

### Finding 3: Critical-Care Airlift Is the Second Constraint

**Critical-care seats on the strategic airlift are the second constraint, and at high intensity only more of them, flown more often, keep up.** Each weekly C-17A sortie carries 90 places, 36 equipped for critical care and 54 standard. At moderate intensity critical-care demand is about <!-- GEN cell:airlift_collapse::Critical, 0%::Mean utilisation::full -->0.83<!-- /GEN --> of the critical places flown. The route keeps pace, but with little margin: it falls behind in <!-- GEN cell:airlift_collapse::Critical, 0%::Collapsed::full -->1 of 30<!-- /GEN --> campaigns, and in <!-- GEN cell:airlift_collapse::Critical, 15%::Collapsed::full -->7 of 30<!-- /GEN --> if 15% of sorties are lost ([Results: national support base and strategic airlift](Results.md#national-support-base-and-strategic-airlift)). At high intensity critical-care demand is <!-- GEN cell:airlift_collapse_high::Critical, 0%::Mean utilisation::full -->2.65<!-- /GEN --> times the critical places flown, while standard places fly part-empty. The critical-care backlog grows all year to about <!-- GEN cell:airlift_places_high::Critical airlift closing backlog::36/54, 7 days (shipped)::sig2 -->2,500<!-- /GEN --> casualties, and a critical casualty waits on average about <!-- GEN cell:airlift_places_high::Critical airlift mean wait (days)::36/54, 7 days (shipped)::sig2 -->110<!-- /GEN --> days for a seat.<sup>[note 3](#notes)</sup> Neither change alone fixes it. Configuring more of the 90 places for critical care while flying weekly leaves the route behind in every campaign. Flying every three days with the shipped 36 critical places leaves it behind in <!-- GEN cell:airlift_places_high::Critical airlift campaigns collapsed::36/54, 3 days::mean -->29<!-- /GEN --> of 30. Flying every three days with at least 45 of the 90 places configured for critical care keeps the route level in every campaign, cuts the critical wait to about <!-- GEN cell:airlift_places_high::Critical airlift mean wait (days)::45/45, 3 days::dp0 -->2<!-- /GEN --> days and prevents about <!-- GEN cell:airlift_places_paired_high::Died of wounds::45/45, 3 days::abs_dp0 -->176<!-- /GEN --> deaths of wounds per campaign. The price falls on the national support base, which then receives casualties sooner: its peak bed demand rises from about <!-- GEN cell:airlift_places_high::Role 4 peak census::36/54, 7 days (shipped)::sig2 -->280<!-- /GEN --> to about <!-- GEN cell:airlift_places_high::Role 4 peak census::45/45, 3 days::sig2 -->530<!-- /GEN --> ([Results: critical-care places per sortie](Results.md#critical-care-places-per-sortie)).

### Finding 4: Delaying Forward Evacuation Costs Lives That Capacity Further Back Cannot Recover

### Finding 8: The Evacuation Policy, the Ambulance Fleet and Role 4

**Three further levers are settled by the evidence: the evacuation policy is a trade the planner chooses, the ambulance fleet is right at three (four at high intensity), and the national support base must be planned as a sustained load of beds.**

*Evacuation policy.* The theatre evacuation policy, how long a casualty may recover in theatre before strategic evacuation, is a command decision with a price at each setting. At moderate intensity a 30-day policy instead of the shipped 21 returns about <!-- GEN cell:policy_establishment_moderate_paired::30 d, 30 beds::Returns to duty::abs_sig1 -->300<!-- /GEN --> more casualties to duty per campaign (about <!-- GEN cell:policy_establishment_moderate::30 d, 30 beds::Returns to duty::sig3 -->2,360<!-- /GEN --> against <!-- GEN cell:policy_establishment_moderate::21 d, 30 beds::Returns to duty::sig3 -->2,060<!-- /GEN -->). It does so without a measurable change in deaths of wounds, and it lowers the peak demand on the national support base from about <!-- GEN cell:policy_establishment_moderate::21 d, 30 beds::Role 4 peak beds::dp0 -->209<!-- /GEN --> beds to about <!-- GEN cell:policy_establishment_moderate::30 d, 30 beds::Role 4 peak beds::dp0 -->195<!-- /GEN -->. The price is paid in the standard of care at R2E. The holding beds are already full, so the casualties kept longer in theatre wait for them: at the shipped 30 beds, the casualties waiting for a holding bed rise from about <!-- GEN cell:policy_establishment_moderate::21 d, 30 beds::R2E hold mean queue::dp0 -->24<!-- /GEN --> to about <!-- GEN cell:policy_establishment_moderate::30 d, 30 beds::R2E hold mean queue::dp0 -->43<!-- /GEN -->, and the operated casualties in overflow from about <!-- GEN cell:policy::R2E post-operative overflow census::21 d (shipped)::dp1 -->5.7<!-- /GEN --> to about <!-- GEN cell:policy::R2E post-operative overflow census::30 d::dp1 -->6.1<!-- /GEN -->. Sixty holding beds bring the holding queue at a 30-day policy back to about <!-- GEN cell:policy_establishment_moderate::30 d, 60 beds::R2E hold mean queue::dp0 -->24<!-- /GEN -->, the shipped level. ## Recommendations in Priority Order

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

**Recommendations are split by whether the remedy is measured, and ranked within each group by how directly the measured effect improves the health outcome of the force.** Act now holds the levers whose effect is measured and resolved against a comparator. Test first holds the levers whose diagnosis is measured but whose remedy is untested or whose effect on outcomes is unresolved. Within a group a lever that moves returns to duty, deaths of wounds or access to intensive care ranks above one that moves only a queue, and a lever addressing the principal constraint ranks above one better measured but acting where the system is not failing. The findings above carry the measurements; each recommendation states what to do, the evidence label it carries, and what the findings leave for the planner to decide.

### Act Now

#### 1. Add Surgical Sections at R2E and R2B, and a Third Theatre Beyond a Fourth R2E Section

**Surgical team hours are the capacity to add, at both R2B and R2E, and at high intensity an added section at either changes deaths of wounds by more than any other lever measured.** **Evidence for the diagnosis: measured. Evidence for the effect of added sections: measured, and resolved for deaths of wounds at high intensity. Evidence for the best means of sourcing the hours: untested.**

The diagnosis and the effect of each added section are [finding 2](#finding-2-surgical-team-hours-at-r2e-are-the-first-constraint) and [finding 7](#finding-7-r2b-sends-most-of-its-surgery-rearward): a fourth R2E section and a second section per R2B unit each prevent a few hundred deaths of wounds per campaign at high intensity, and beyond a fourth R2E section a third theatre is needed for a third section on each shift. Prehospital transport to a surgical facility within 60 minutes was among the interventions associated with the fall in case fatality across the Afghanistan and Iraq conflicts [[8]](#references), so a casualty who travels further to reach a surgeon because none is rostered is a clinical cost rather than an administrative one. Whether the hours are a standing establishment or a surge measure depends on the intensity planned for: at high intensity the R2E theatre queue stands empty for <!-- GEN cell:queue_clearance::R2E operating theatres::High: queue empty::full -->7%<!-- /GEN --> of the campaign, so the hours must be permanently established, while at moderate intensity it stands empty for <!-- GEN cell:queue_clearance::R2E operating theatres::Moderate: queue empty::full -->85%<!-- /GEN --> and the hours can be held as a capability brought to bear on the heavy day ([Results: queue behaviour over the campaign](Results.md#queue-behaviour-over-the-campaign)). Extending coverage needs no additional principal equipment, but it does need provision in the operational viability period, the organisational design and the workforce model, and extending the rostered teams' hours and fielding additional teams deliver coverage at different costs in personnel, sustainment and clinical risk, a question the simulation does not answer. The added sections move the pressure onto the post-operative beds, and at high intensity twelve R2E intensive care beds take it back where more holding beds do not ([finding 6](#finding-6-adding-surgery-moves-the-pressure-onto-the-post-operative-beds)), so the beds belong in the same plan as the sections; a second R2B theatre is the next forward constraint and is untested ([Research Agenda](#research-agenda)).

#### 2. Fly More Critical-Care Airlift Places More Often at High Intensity

**Strategic airlift is limited by its critical-care places, and at high intensity only more of them, flown more often, settle it.** **Evidence for the diagnosis: measured. Evidence for the remedy: measured; at moderate intensity no airlift arm resolves a change in deaths of wounds.**

The diagnosis and the remedy are [finding 3](#finding-3-critical-care-airlift-is-the-second-constraint): at high intensity forty-five of the 90 places configured for critical care and flown every three days keep the critical route level, where either change alone does not. The critical wait is set by the places flown rather than by the beds that hold the casualty meanwhile: at high intensity sixteen R2E intensive care beds leave the critical-route wait at <!-- GEN cell:icu_bed_high::Critical-route evacuation wait (days)::16 beds::dp0 -->111<!-- /GEN --> days against <!-- GEN cell:icu_bed_high::Critical-route evacuation wait (days)::4 beds (shipped)::dp0 -->110<!-- /GEN --> at the shipped four ([Results: R2E intensive care bed establishment](Results.md#r2e-intensive-care-bed-establishment)). How far a real sortie can be reconfigured is not stated by an open-access source, so a planner should confirm the critical-care configuration of the airframe, and the critical-care teams to crew it, before relying on it ([note 3](#notes)). The faster lift is paid for in the national support base's beds ([finding 8](#finding-8-the-evacuation-policy-the-ambulance-fleet-and-role-4)).

#### 3. Hold the Evacuation Policy as a Decision and Keep the Sorties Weekly and Reliable

**The theatre evacuation policy is a command decision with a price at each setting, and at moderate intensity the shipped establishment carries a 30-day policy without saturating, at the price of a longer wait for a holding bed.** Doctrine names it the theatre patient return policy, a command decision balancing each casualty's needs against keeping enough treatment and evacuation capacity available to the force [[6]](#references) (para. 1.55), so [finding 8](#finding-8-the-evacuation-policy-the-ambulance-fleet-and-role-4) gives the price of each setting rather than a single answer: a longer policy returns more casualties to duty and lowers the demand on the national support base, and pays for it in casualties waiting for an R2E holding bed. The one in-theatre figure in the historical record of return to duty ([system reference](../README.md#return-to-duty)) sits at the shipped 21 days, which retains <!-- GEN cell:policy::In-theatre share (%)::21 d (shipped)::dp0 -->40<!-- /GEN -->% of casualties in theatre, while a 30-day policy retains <!-- GEN cell:policy::In-theatre share (%)::30 d::dp0 -->49<!-- /GEN -->% ([Results: evacuation policy](Results.md#evacuation-policy)); retention above that figure is not evidence against a 30-day policy, but the returns to duty it buys rest on uncalibrated recovery factors ([note 8](#notes)). Whether a 45 or 60-day policy holds the pools at moderate intensity is not tested, the grid stopping at 30 days. **Evidence: measured.**

**The sortie schedule is a measured lever: fly at least weekly, and protect the sorties from loss.** Lengthening the interval lengthens the wait, from <!-- GEN cell:airlift_interval::Mean wait (days)::7 days (shipped)::dp1 -->4.9<!-- /GEN --> days at seven to <!-- GEN cell:airlift_interval::Mean wait (days)::14 days::dp1 -->41.6<!-- /GEN --> at fourteen, and the share of R2E holding beds held by casualties awaiting airlift from <!-- GEN cell:airlift_interval::Share of R2E holding beds held by the evacuation wait::7 days (shipped)::mean -->10%<!-- /GEN --> to <!-- GEN cell:airlift_interval::Share of R2E holding beds held by the evacuation wait::14 days::mean -->34%<!-- /GEN -->, so a lever that delays a sortie propagates into the beds; the loss of sorties is priced in [finding 3](#finding-3-critical-care-airlift-is-the-second-constraint) ([Results: national support base and strategic airlift](Results.md#national-support-base-and-strategic-airlift)). **Evidence: measured.**

**Plan the national support base as a sustained load of beds and surgery, derived from the evacuation pipeline rather than from a casualty estimate.** [Finding 8](#finding-8-the-evacuation-policy-the-ambulance-fleet-and-role-4) gives the beds. The base also owes surgery: about <!-- GEN cell:role4_operations::Operations owed by casualties admitted during the campaign::Moderate intensity::sig3 -->1,220<!-- /GEN --> operations per campaign at moderate intensity and <!-- GEN cell:role4_operations::Operations owed by casualties admitted during the campaign::High intensity::sig3 -->2,050<!-- /GEN --> at high, debridements the largest share, and about <!-- GEN cell:role4_operations::Operations owed on the busiest day::Moderate intensity::dp0 -->21<!-- /GEN --> and <!-- GEN cell:role4_operations::Operations owed on the busiest day::High intensity::dp0 -->28<!-- /GEN --> on the busiest day ([Results: Role 4 bed demand](Results.md#role-4-bed-demand)), so the base is sized in operating theatres as well as beds. **Evidence: measured.**

#### 4. Size the Medical Evacuation Fleet at Three Ambulances, Four at High Intensity, and Four Trucks

**Three ambulances carry the inter-echelon load between R1, R2B and R2E at moderate intensity, and four at high.** The queue and the fourth vehicle's effect are in [finding 8](#finding-8-the-evacuation-policy-the-ambulance-fleet-and-role-4), with the idle R2E evacuation sections and trucks that point to personnel who could be reallocated, a reallocation that is untested. ### Planning for Moderate Intensity

**Planning against moderate intensity, the shipped system keeps pace, so act on the policy, airlift and fleet recommendations, hold the surgical hours as a capability brought to bear on the heavy day, and decide how much degraded care to accept.** Every queue settles ([finding 1](#finding-1-the-system-keeps-pace-at-moderate-intensity-and-not-at-high)), but the R2E beds run full and most post-definitive intensive care is given outside an intensive care bed ([finding 5](#finding-5-the-r2e-beds-are-full-and-the-cost-is-paid-in-care)); more beds buy care rather than lives the model can measure. A second R2B surgical section per unit keeps most surgery forward ([finding 7](#finding-7-r2b-sends-most-of-its-surgery-rearward)), and a 30-day evacuation policy is a trade the shipped establishment can carry ([finding 8](#finding-8-the-evacuation-policy-the-ambulance-fleet-and-role-4)). **Evidence: measured for the pathway and for the diversions the second R2B section removes; unresolved for outcomes.**

### Planning for High Intensity

**Planning against high intensity, add surgical sections at R2E and R2B and the R2E intensive care beds behind them, and fly more critical-care airlift places more often; no establishment of beds or evacuation policy settles the system while the airlift falls behind.** The surgical teams and the critical-care airlift are the two constraints that decide the outcome ([finding 2](#finding-2-surgical-team-hours-at-r2e-are-the-first-constraint), [finding 3](#finding-3-critical-care-airlift-is-the-second-constraint)), adding surgery moves the pressure onto the post-operative beds ([finding 6](#finding-6-adding-surgery-moves-the-pressure-onto-the-post-operative-beds)), and the national support base receives its load during the campaign only if the airlift keeps pace ([finding 8](#finding-8-the-evacuation-policy-the-ambulance-fleet-and-role-4)). Forward holding is the lever to withhold, no forward arm resolving a change in deaths of wounds. Every high-intensity mortality figure describes a force that adds neither surgical nor airlift capacity over the year ([Limitations](#limitations)). **Evidence: measured for the surgical sections, the airlift shortfall and its remedy.**

### What Resourcing Alone Does Not Fix, and What Is Not Supported

**Forward holding is not supported on the present evidence.** Holding operated casualties forward at R2B does not measurably relieve R2E intensive care at moderate intensity: forward utilisation rises across the windows because the policy is being applied, while the post-operative overflow at R2E is not separable from the arm with none held forward ([Results: forward holding](Results.md#forward-holding-of-post-operative-intensive-care)). ## Research Agenda

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

**The development priorities follow from the levers the simulation cannot yet evaluate or resolve.** Each item below names the decision it would unblock.

| Priority | Development | Decision it unblocks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost surgical sections, and the third theatre a third section on each shift needs, in personnel and sustainment | Whether the sections that shorten the high-intensity standing queue, and the theatre beyond a fourth section, are affordable |
| 2 | Test a second R2B theatre alongside the second R2B section | Whether the forward theatre, once a second section makes it the limit, keeps the casualties it now diverts |
| 3 | Model a critical casualty's deterioration while awaiting airlift, and confirm how far a sortie can be configured for critical care | How many deaths the wait causes, and whether the critical-care places the settled high-intensity arms fly are available |
| 4 | Re-run the unresolved experiments at the replication counts the resolution table states | The hold window's effect on R2E load, and the cost of rationing and of the release |
| 5 | Sweep the R2B diversion thresholds across their range | Where to strike the trade between waiting forward and transferring load rearward |
| 6 | Test policies for recovering holding capacity during a casualty surge event, and the triggers for applying them | How to relieve the reversal of the intensive care and holding pathways under surge |
| 7 | Casualty severity conditioning of surgery durations | Whether theatre contention is understated on the heavy days it is measured on |
| 8 | A global screen with common random numbers and a design sized to the replication noise, and a sizing of the R2E establishment against the level its queues reach under a defined surge | Whether parameters interact in ways the one-lever sweeps cannot detect, and whether an informed estimate not yet swept could move a finding ([system reference](../README.md#further-development)) |

Alongside these, the simulated system's design and calibration would benefit from structured review by clinical and health planning subject matter experts. The parameters governing intensive care rationing and post-operative risk are informed estimates rather than measured values, and expert calibration would do more to improve confidence in the mortality findings than additional computation.

## Limitations

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

Six limitations bear on how the recommendations above should be read.

**The simulation is verified but not validated.** It behaves as its specification describes, which is a separate question from whether that specification represents the real trauma system well [[9]](#references). Verification is reported in [Annex A of the results paper](Results.md#annex-a-model-verification-of-one-seed-42-campaign). The treated-cohort died-of-wounds rates are reported in [Results](Results.md#treated-cohort-mortality) at each anchor's horizon and at 360 days: the shipped configuration's rate is <!-- GEN cell:dow_calibration::Shipped default::Rate at the anchor's horizon::full -->0.421% [0.350%, 0.492%]<!-- /GEN --> at the 30 days its Ajax Bay anchor describes and <!-- GEN cell:dow_calibration::Shipped default::Rate at 360 days::full -->0.541% [0.453%, 0.629%]<!-- /GEN --> at 360 days, because under a model in which every hour of delay carries risk a longer campaign accumulates more of it. Every lever above is a lever inside the simulation, and holds only as far as the simulation does.

**Compute time limited what could be measured.** The effects in the resolution table are unresolved for that reason, for the rarity of the event, or because they fail the Holm adjustment that guards against reading one chance difference in twenty as real, and the replication counts that would settle them are stated there. This constrains the precision of the findings rather than their direction, and it bears hardest on mortality, which is the rarest quantity the simulation reports.

**Comparisons between two configurations are not controlled.** Changing a setting alters the sequence of random draws, so the two arms of a comparison generate different casualty streams and cannot be matched campaign for campaign; their responses are uncorrelated within replication ([Methods: Pairing Arms on a Control Seed](Methods.md#pairing-arms-on-a-control-seed)). The effect is a loss of precision rather than a bias, which is why several comparisons are unresolved at the replication counts used. The theatre sweep carries a null control that shows its size: at three sections a third theatre cannot be used, two sections on shift never outnumbering three theatres, yet that arm changes deaths of wounds at high intensity by <!-- GEN cell:ot_bed_paired_high::Died of wounds::3 theatres, 3 sections::full -->−79.07 [−135.37, −22.76]†<!-- /GEN -->, larger than the resolved <!-- GEN cell:ot_bed_paired_high::Died of wounds::3 theatres, 6 sections against 2 theatres, 6 sections::full -->−37.60 [−52.42, −22.78]<!-- /GEN --> for a third theatre at six sections ([Results: R2E operating theatre establishment](Results.md#r2e-operating-theatre-establishment)). A single high-intensity mortality difference of that order is therefore read against the divergence of the casualty streams, and the paper rests its mortality recommendations on differences that survive Holm's adjustment and move consistently across the arms of a sweep. The casualty intensity comparison is unaffected, its arms differing by design rather than by a small perturbation.

**The high-intensity airlift figures measure a backlog still growing, not a level.** The sustained-horizon measurement shows the casualties awaiting airlift still growing at the end of a year, and at moderate intensity possibly rising slowly, a classification its interval only just supports ([Results: sustained operations](Results.md#sustained-operations)), so no airlift figure is a steady-state figure. Nothing recommended here depends on that level, the conclusion drawn being that the backlog does not clear. The high-intensity theatre queue, by contrast, settles at a standing level.

**High intensity is a projection of a force that does not adapt.** The establishment, the surgical roster and the weekly airlift stay as configured however far demand outruns them, with no expectant category, no surgical reinforcement and no airlift added (README Further Development L45). The surgical and evacuation queues therefore keep building, deaths of wounds per day are classified <!-- GEN cell:long_horizon_stability::Deaths of wounds per day::High intensity::full -->**drifting, +5.1%/block**, 0.5 to 2.2<!-- /GEN -->, and the treated-cohort rate is <!-- GEN cell:dow_calibration::High intensity::Rate at the anchor's horizon::full -->2.905% [2.681%, 3.128%]<!-- /GEN --> at the 82 days of the Okinawa anchor against <!-- GEN cell:dow_calibration::High intensity::Rate at 360 days::full -->5.692% [5.088%, 6.295%]<!-- /GEN --> at 360 days ([Results: treated-cohort mortality](Results.md#treated-cohort-mortality); [sustained operations](Results.md#sustained-operations)). A high-intensity mortality figure is the cost of a year without adapting, not the expected outcome of a high-intensity campaign; the surgical section, operating theatre and sortie interval sweeps measure what three forms of adaptation buy ([Results: surgical team establishment](Results.md#surgical-team-establishment); [Results: R2E operating theatre establishment](Results.md#r2e-operating-theatre-establishment); [Results](Results.md#high-intensity-strategic-sortie-interval-by-policy-and-holding-establishment)).

**The risk of waiting for airlift is understated.** A critical casualty awaiting a seat is held in a holding bed, or in overflow when none is free, at the death rate of post-operative intensive care, for however long the wait lasts. At high intensity those waits run to months, and a Priority 1 surgical casualty held that long would in reality deteriorate or need further surgery, so the deaths of wounds the airlift shortfall causes in the model are a floor: <!-- GEN cell:scenario_totals::Died of wounds/run::High intensity::mean -->568.60<!-- /GEN --> per run at high intensity against <!-- GEN cell:scenario_totals::Died of wounds/run::Moderate intensity::mean -->21.17<!-- /GEN --> at moderate ([Results](Results.md#comparative-scenario-analysis)). No open source gives the hazard of that situation.

Two narrower caveats apply. Clinical teams are taken whole rather than by individual clinician, so team utilisation overstates scarcity where a procedure needs only part of a team; and one pool of R2E holding beds carries both in-theatre recovery and the strategic evacuation wait, so those queue figures combine two demands. The model's gaps are catalogued in the system reference [[7]](#references).

## Conclusion

<small>[Return to Top](#contents)</small>

This paper set out to identify options for improving the land-based trauma system, to establish whether that system keeps pace as casualty intensity rises and where it fails first, and to state what the evidence supports for each lever a planner controls.

**The shipped system copes at moderate intensity with degraded care and fails at high intensity, where R2E surgical teams and critical-care airlift are the major constraints.** At moderate intensity every queue settles, but the R2E beds run full and most intensive care after definitive surgery is given outside an intensive care bed ([finding 1](#finding-1-the-system-keeps-pace-at-moderate-intensity-and-not-at-high), [finding 5](#finding-5-the-r2e-beds-are-full-and-the-cost-is-paid-in-care)). At high intensity wounded in action rise <!-- GEN cell:scenario_totals::Wounded in action/run::Ratio::sig2 -->3.6×<!-- /GEN -->, a standing queue of about <!-- GEN cell:scenario_queue::R2E operating theatre::High intensity mean queue::dp0 -->73<!-- /GEN --> casualties forms for the R2E surgical teams, and the critical-care airlift falls behind all year ([Results: comparative scenario analysis](Results.md#comparative-scenario-analysis)). The surgical constraint is team hours rather than theatre space, and the airlift constraint is the critical-care places, which the standard places cannot substitute for ([finding 2](#finding-2-surgical-team-hours-at-r2e-are-the-first-constraint), [finding 3](#finding-3-critical-care-airlift-is-the-second-constraint)).

**The recommendations are split by whether the remedy is measured.** Act now: add surgical sections, a fourth R2E section and a second R2B section per unit, with a third R2E theatre where three sections share a shift, and plan twelve R2E intensive care beds with them at high intensity, since added surgery moves the pressure onto the post-operative beds and more holding beds do not relieve it; fly 45 of the 90 airlift places for critical care every three days at high intensity; hold the evacuation policy as a command decision, read from its price, keep the sorties at least weekly and protected from loss, and size the national support base in operating theatres as well as beds; and keep three ambulances, four at high intensity, and four trucks. Test first: ten R2B holding beds per unit, which relieve R2E holding though the forward queue needs none; the pre-open window; the forward surgical saturation release, which the added sections partly replace; and intensive care beds beyond those, which improve care without a resolved effect on deaths of wounds. Do not deliver post-operative intensive care forward under sustained saturation, no forward holding arm resolving a change in deaths of wounds. Every recommendation assumes forward evacuation is uncontested; a contested axis adds deaths forward that no Role 2 establishment recovers ([finding 4](#finding-4-delaying-forward-evacuation-costs-lives-that-capacity-further-back-cannot-recover)).

**Further research would be worthwhile, and in three directions.** The most valuable is pricing the surgical sections, and the theatre and post-operative beds they bring with them, in sustainment as well as personnel, the lever table stating only personnel and principal equipment. The second is the critical casualty in transit: the deterioration of a casualty awaiting airlift, how far a sortie can be configured for critical care, and the prolonged care a contested forward axis would need, each resting on an informed estimate. The third is structured expert review of the simulated system's design and calibration, which would do more for confidence in the mortality findings than any amount of additional computation.
